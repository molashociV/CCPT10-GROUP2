Sequences and Polynomials

Understanding Mathematical Patterns Through Real-Life Examples

What is a SEQUENCE?

A sequence is an ordered list of numbers, objects, or other elements that follow a particular pattern or rule. Unlike a set, the order of elements in a sequence matters.

What is a POLYNOMIAL?

A polynomial is a mathematical expression made by adding and subtracting terms, where each term is a constant or a constant multiplied by a variable raised to a non-negative whole number exponent.

Example 1: Sequence

A patient is advised to walk for 10 minutes on the first day as part of a gradual recovery program. The walking time increases by 5 minutes each day. How many minutes should the patient walk on the 7th day?

Solution

This is an arithmetic sequence because the walking time increases by the same amount each day.

Given:

  • First term: a₁ = 10 minutes
  • Common difference: d = 5 minutes
  • Term needed: n = 7

Formula:

aₙ = a₁ + (n − 1)d

Substitute:

a₇ = 10 + (7 − 1)(5)
a₇ = 10 + 30
a₇ = 40 minutes
Therefore, the patient would walk 40 minutes on the 7th day.

Example 2: Sequence

A small business sells 500 products in its first month. Because of a steady increase in customers, the business expects to sell 50 more products each month. How many products does the business expect to sell in the 8th month?

Solution

This is an arithmetic sequence because the number of products increases by the same amount each month.

Given:

  • First term: a₁ = 500
  • Common difference: d = 50
  • Number of terms: n = 8

Formula:

aₙ = a₁ + (n − 1)d

Substitute:

a₈ = 500 + (8 − 1)(50)
a₈ = 500 + 7(50)
a₈ = 500 + 350
a₈ = 850
Thus, the business expects to sell 850 products in the 8th month, assuming the increase of 50 products per month continues.

Example 3: Sequence

A farmer harvests 200 kilograms of tomatoes from a field during the first harvest. Because of improved crop growth and management, the farmer expects the harvest to increase by 25 kilograms for each succeeding harvest. How many kilograms are expected during the 6th harvest?

Solution

This is an arithmetic sequence because the harvest increases by the same amount each time.

Given:

  • First term: a₁ = 200 kg
  • Common difference: d = 25 kg
  • Term needed: n = 6

Formula:

aₙ = a₁ + (n − 1)d

Substitute:

a₆ = 200 + (6 − 1)(25)
a₆ = 200 + 125
a₆ = 325 kg
Thus, the farmer expects to harvest 325 kilograms of tomatoes during the 6th harvest, assuming the increase remains constant.

Example 4: Sequence

An engineer is testing a simplified model of a structure where the measured vibration amplitude decreases by 20% after each successive test. If the amplitude during the first test is 50 mm, what is the expected amplitude during the 5th test?

Solution

This is a geometric sequence because each term is multiplied by the same ratio.

Given:

  • First term: a₁ = 50 mm
  • Common ratio: r = 0.80 because 100% − 20% = 80%
  • Number of terms: n = 5

Formula:

aₙ = a₁rn−1

Substitute:

a₅ = 50(0.80)5−1
a₅ = 50(0.80)4
a₅ = 50(0.4096)
a₅ = 20.48 mm
Therefore, the expected vibration amplitude during the 5th test is 20.48 mm, according to this simplified model.

Real-Life Applications in Disaster Management

What is a SEQUENCE?

EX. 1: SEQUENCE

Problem: During a typhoon evacuation, a community center helps 50 people in the first hour. Because more volunteers arrive, the number of people assisted increases by 20 people every hour. How many people will be assisted in the 5th hour?

SOLUTION:

GIVEN:

a₁ = 50

d = 20

n = 5

FORMULA:

aₙ = a₁ + (n − 1)d

SUBSTITUTE:

a₅ = 50 + (5 − 1)(20)

a₅ = 50 + 80

a₅ = 130

Therefore, the community center can assist 130 people during the 5th hour.

EX. 2: SEQUENCE

Problem: A relief center distributes 100 food packs on the first day. Because more families arrive, it distributes 25 additional packs each day. How many food packs will be distributed on the 7th day?

SOLUTION:

FORMULA:

aₙ = a₁ + (n − 1)d

SUBSTITUTE:

a₇ = 100 + (7 − 1)(25)

a₇ = 100 + 6(25)

a₇ = 100 + 150

a₇ = 250

Thus, the relief center will distribute 250 food packs on the 7th day.

EX. 3: SEQUENCE

Problem: After heavy rainfall, a monitoring station records the floodwater level decreasing by 8 cm every hour. If the initial level is 80 cm, what will the level be after 5 hours?

SOLUTION:

FORMULA:

aₙ = a₁ + (n − 1)d

SUBSTITUTE:

a₆ = 80 + (6 − 1)(−8)

a₆ = 80 − 40

a₆ = 40 cm

Thus, the water level will be 40 cm after 5 hours.

EX. 1: POLYNOMIAL

Problem: A model estimates the area affected by a landslide as P(x) = x² + 6x + 20; x = 4.

SOLUTION:

P(x) = x² + 6x + 20

P(4) = (4)² + 6(4) + 20

P(4) = 16 + 24 + 20

P(4) = 60

Thus, the affected area is 60 square units.
